Overview

What Plaid does

Plaid operates a data and payments network that lets applications connect to users' financial accounts. Its products power thousands of fintechs, several Fortune 500 companies and large banks, covering roughly 12,000 financial institutions across the US, Canada, UK and Europe.

Plaid was founded in 2013 by Zach Perret and William Hockey. It is privately held and has raised about $1.3 billion across six rounds, most recently a $575M round in April 2025 at about a $6.1bn valuation. A planned $5bn acquisition by Visa (announced 2020) was abandoned in 2021 after US DOJ antitrust concerns. Sacra reports Plaid was weighing a US IPO as of July 2026.

What it sells

Services

Plaid's products fall into account connectivity, data, identity, risk and payments.

Auth

Account-number and routing verification for linking bank accounts and payments.

Data products

Transactions, Investments, Liabilities, Balance, Assets and Enrich (transaction enrichment).

Identity & monitoring

Identity Verification (16,000+ ID types across 200 countries and territories), KYC and fraud monitoring.

Signal

Risk and fraud scoring of transactions.

Transfer / Payment Initiation

Initiating and managing account-to-account payments.

Plaid Link

The consumer-facing account-linking widget.

How it earns

Revenue streams

Plaid's revenue is usage-based: customers pay for API access. It publishes no revenue split.

Official pricing models

  • One-time fees per connected Item (Auth, Identity)
  • Monthly subscription per Item (Transactions, Liabilities, Investments)
  • Per-request fees (Balance, Signal) and flexible per-request or per-Item fees (Assets, Statements, Enrich)
  • Separate models for Payment Initiation, Consumer Report, Transfer, Monitor and Identity Verification

Mix shift (third-party view)

  • Sacra: legacy bank connectivity is still the majority of ARR; payments, anti-fraud and underwriting passed 20% of ARR in 2024
  • Plaid's CFO said revenue from credit, anti-fraud and payments more than doubled in 2025
  • A precise percentage split is not found in sources
