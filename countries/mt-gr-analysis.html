Overview

A late MiCA starter next to an established hub

Greece granted its first MiCA crypto licences only in July 2026 — to two small domestic firms — while Malta already hosts 22 CASPs including several of the world's largest exchanges.

On 22 July 2026 the Hellenic Capital Market Commission (HCMC) authorised Xenios Blockchain Group and Capital Wallet Greece. The Greek process drew international attention when Binance withdrew its Greek MiCA application on 24 June 2026; the Wall Street Journal later reported, citing unnamed sources, that ECB President Christine Lagarde had pressed the Greek government against approval. On payments, Greece is dominated by Viva Payment Services, the Bank of Greece's first licensed EMI.

Regulatory landscape

Who supervises what

Malta

  • MFSA licenses CASPs, EMIs and PIs; FIAU supervises AML.
  • VFA Act (2018) replaced by MiCA; transitional period ran to 1 July 2026.

Greece

  • HCMC authorises and supervises CASPs; the Bank of Greece supervises ART/EMT issuers, EMIs and PIs.
  • Law 5193/2025 (11 April 2025) implements MiCA and adds civil and criminal sanctions for unlicensed activity.
  • Pre-MiCA: VASPs registered with the HCMC under AML Law 4557/2018 (Decision 5/898/2020). The HCMC reported only a handful of registrations (five in 2023).
  • Transition end: sources differ — some cite 30 December 2025, others the EU-wide 1 July 2026 backstop.

Binance's withdrawn application

  • Binance applied to the HCMC for MiCA authorisation and said it believed the review was complete; it withdrew the application on 24 June 2026 and said it would seek authorisation in another EU state.
  • The WSJ (18 Sep 2026) reported that ECB President Lagarde pressed Prime Minister Mitsotakis against approval, citing Binance's 2023 US guilty plea and dollar-stablecoin risks. Not confirmed by the ECB or HCMC; under MiCA the licensing decision rests with the HCMC.

Other exchanges previously served Greece through HCMC VASP registrations — notably Crypto.com and Gemini (see Common companies).

Greece — payments

Bank of Greece-licensed payment players

Five EMIs operate in Greece; one, Viva, dominates and passports across Europe.

Viva Payment Services S.A.

EMI licence no. 1 (23 Jul 2018). Card processing, merchant IBAN accounts and terminals; branches across the EU per the DIAS participant list. 2023: total assets €527m, net loss €24.5m.

Cosmote Payments

EMI of the Cosmote / OTE telecoms group; largest domestic EMI by assets among the smaller four (≈ €57m).

Everypay

Payment services; ≈ €38m total assets.

Tora Wallet

E-wallet EMI; ≈ €26m total assets.

Otro Pay

Bill-payment EMI authorised 21 Mar 2024; passported to 6 EEA states.

Passport-only overlap

  • OKX, Gate and other Malta CASPs can serve Greece by MiCA passport; no Greek entity identified.
  • Greek CASPs into Malta: Capital Wallet Greece has announced a phased EU expansion; no Malta passport confirmed yet.

What this tells us

  • This is the first country pair where the overlap runs from the other country into Malta: Viva, a Greek EMI, appears with a Malta presence.
  • For crypto, overlap mirrors Italy and the Netherlands — Malta-licensed exchanges that had also registered locally before MiCA.
  • Viva's Malta listing comes from a payment-system participant list; confirm the branch or passport status on the MFSA register.

Opportunity lens (analysis, not sourced fact)

  • First-year Greek CASPs: Capital Wallet Greece and Xenios face their first MiCA statutory audits, safeguarding reviews and DORA ICT assessments.
  • Pipeline: with only two Greek authorisations, further applicants — and firms relocating from Greece after the Binance episode — are likely to need readiness and gap-assessment support.
  • Viva: a large, pan-European EMI with a Malta presence suits coordinated Deloitte Greece – Deloitte Malta work on safeguarding, SOC reporting and PSD3 readiness, subject to independence checks.
