Overview

What Blockchain.com does

Blockchain.com began life in 2011 as Blockchain.info, one of the first Bitcoin block explorers, built by Ben Reeves in York, England. Nicolas Cary and Peter Smith joined shortly after, and the trio grew the project from a transaction-lookup tool into a hybrid custodial/non-custodial wallet, then a full trading exchange, rebranding to Blockchain.com in December 2019. The platform has facilitated over $1 trillion in cumulative crypto transactions and has created more than 94 million wallets across over 200 countries.

The group's holding structure is commonly cited as Blockchain Luxembourg S.A., with operating entities in the UK, US, Singapore, Cayman Islands, Lithuania and, since late 2025, Malta. Blockchain.com has raised roughly $600-720 million in venture funding to date (Series E, led by Kingsway Capital) and was valued at approximately £5 billion in 2020, though it remains private with no audited consolidated financials publicly available.

What it sells

Services

Blockchain.com's product line spans its original block explorer, retail wallet and exchange products, and an institutional trading and custody business.

Blockchain Explorer

The company's founding product — a free, public tool for browsing Bitcoin (and later other chains') transactions, blocks and network statistics.

Blockchain.com Wallet (DeFi Wallet)

A non-custodial wallet giving users sole control of their private keys and a recovery phrase; supports direct peer-to-peer trading via an integrated DEX aggregator across Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum and Optimism.

Blockchain.com Account

A custodial account offering fiat on/off-ramps, Swap, and Rewards — a passive-yield product — alongside simplified buying, selling and holding of crypto.

Blockchain.com Exchange

Launched in 2019, an institutional-grade trading venue with a low-latency matching engine, spot and margin trading, and 70+ trading pairs in USD, EUR and GBP.

Institutional services

OTC trading, custody, and credit facilities for protocols, funds and high-volume investors — over $8 billion in credit extended to date.

Market data & analytics

APIs and dashboards built on the Explorer's underlying data, used by institutional clients for on-chain analysis and pricing.

How it earns

Revenue streams

Blockchain.com monetises across its retail and institutional product lines, though — unlike listed peers — it does not publish an audited, itemised revenue breakdown.

Core streams

  • Exchange trading fees on spot and margin transactions
  • Premium/subscription wallet services
  • Spreads and interest on institutional OTC trading, custody and credit facilities
  • Institutional data and analytics products built on the Explorer's infrastructure

Disclosure basis

  • As a private company with a documented history of delayed UK statutory filings, Blockchain.com does not publish a current, audited consolidated revenue figure
  • Third-party estimates vary widely and are not verified against filed accounts; a commonly cited figure (~$23 million) appears to reflect only a small subsidiary rather than group-wide revenue

Because Blockchain.com's core wallet is explicitly non-custodial, the company structurally separates a large share of its user base (wallet-only users) from its revenue-generating custodial and institutional products — a business-model distinction worth noting alongside any revenue estimate.

Where it operates

Markets

Blockchain.com has assembled a patchwork of licences across major markets, most recently consolidating its EU presence under a single MiCA authorisation.

Regulatory footprint

  • European Economic Area — MiCA licence via Malta entity Blue Cube (Malta) Ltd, effective 4 December 2025, covering all 30 EEA states
  • Singapore — Major Payment Institution licence from the Monetary Authority of Singapore (Aug 2023)
  • Cayman Islands — registered Virtual Asset Service Provider (VASP) with CIMA
  • Italy — registered with OAM as a crypto service provider (Aug 2022); Lithuania — registered VASP
  • United States — state-level money transmitter licences (no New York BitLicense); relies on a Bakkt partnership to reach all 50 states

Recent expansion & retrenchment

  • Physical office opened in Nigeria (May 2025), with service extended to Ghana, targeting African crypto adoption
  • Monitoring regulatory developments in Kenya and South Africa for further expansion
  • Paused services in Canada in 2023 following restrictive custody and exchange rules
  • Withdrew its direct UK FCA registration application in 2022, prioritising the EU MiCA route instead; UK users are served via FCA-regulated partner Englebert Ltd.

Consolidated financials

No current audited consolidated financial statements identified

Blockchain.com is a privately held company, and its UK filing record has itself become a matter of public record: as of 2024, the last full statutory accounts filed at UK Companies House were made up to 31 December 2020, with 2021 accounts filed roughly two years late. In October 2024, Companies House issued a summons against co-founder and President Nicolas Cary and operations executive Al Turnbull over the delayed filings, under powers strengthened by the UK's Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023. The company has attributed the delay to workforce reductions that took time to stabilise, and stated it was taking steps to remediate its statutory filing compliance.

Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Malta became the seat of Blockchain.com's entire EU regulatory relationship in October 2025, when the company secured a MiCA licence through a newly formed Maltese entity.

Malta as the new EU regulatory hub

  • In October 2025, the Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA) approved Blockchain.com's application under the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA), via its Maltese entity Blue Cube (Malta) Ltd.
  • The licence became effective for existing EEA customers on 4 December 2025, migrating their accounts to be serviced by the Malta entity, with a mandatory account-update process required by 4 February 2026.
  • The single Malta-issued MiCA licence lets Blockchain.com serve all 30 European Economic Area member states under one regulatory regime, replacing a previously fragmented, country-by-country compliance approach.
  • CEO Peter Smith cited Malta's "regulatory clarity" as the reason for the choice, and the company hired Maltese financial-services veteran Fiorentina D'Amore — former chair of the Financial Institutions Malta Association — to lead the Malta entity and the wider EU rollout.

This makes Blockchain.com's Malta relationship one of direct licensing and operational presence, similar in kind to Bitpanda's and OKX's MFSA authorisations — though it is worth noting Malta's MiCA supervisory approach has itself drawn scrutiny from ESMA over its authorisation procedures, a live regulatory debate the MFSA has pushed back on as premature.

The public sources reviewed for this profile — Blockchain.com's own releases, Companies House filings and press coverage of its regulatory history — do not name Deloitte as an auditor, attestor or disclosed advisor to Blockchain.com or any of its subsidiaries, including the new Malta entity.
