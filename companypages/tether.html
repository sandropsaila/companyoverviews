Overview

What Tether does

Tether issues USDT, a digital token designed to always be worth one US dollar. Users deposit dollars with Tether and receive USDT in return; Tether invests those dollars — overwhelmingly in short-term US Treasury bills — and keeps the interest. Because it does not pay yield to USDT holders, and operates with a comparatively small headcount, the business has become one of the most profitable in the world relative to its size.

The company began in 2014 as Realcoin, founded by Brock Pierce, Reeve Collins and Craig Sellars, and was quickly rebranded Tether. It has long been affiliated with the Bitfinex cryptocurrency exchange through a shared parent, iFinex Inc.; the wider corporate group now sits under Tether Holdings Limited, with operating entities including Tether Limited, Tether Operations Limited and Tether International, S.A. de C.V. In 2025 the group relocated its base from the British Virgin Islands to El Salvador, obtaining a licence under that country's Digital Asset Service Provider regime and establishing its first formal head office there.

What it sells

Services

Tether's core product is USDT itself, but the group has diversified into other tokens, a tokenisation platform, and adjacent technology and commodity investments.

USDT (Tether)

The flagship US dollar–pegged stablecoin, issued across more than a dozen blockchains including Tron, Ethereum and Solana; the primary source of liquidity across crypto trading and, increasingly, cross-border payments.

USAT

A newly launched, US-domiciled dollar token for American businesses and institutions, issued via Anchorage Digital Bank with Cantor Fitzgerald as reserve custodian, built to operate under the US GENIUS Act framework — led by Tether U.S. CEO Bo Hines.

Tether Gold (XAU₮)

A token representing ownership of physical gold bullion held in vaults; Tether has continued to add to its own gold holdings alongside this product.

Hadron by Tether

A proprietary tokenisation platform used to issue other digital assets — including USAT — and integrated with third-party compliance tools such as Chainalysis.

Other currency-pegged tokens

MXN₮ (Mexican peso) launched 2022; euro (EUR₮) and offshore yuan (CNH₮) tokens issued in earlier years, with EUR₮ retired in November 2024 rather than adapted for EU rules.

Adjacent ventures

Bitcoin mining and infrastructure investment, an artificial-intelligence push (Tether.ai, the QVAC platform), and a large equity stake in Bitcoin miner Northern Data (rebranded as an AI infrastructure play).

How it earns

Revenue streams

Tether's business model is simple relative to its scale: it earns from what its dollar reserves generate, not from fees paid by everyday USDT users.

Core streams

  • Interest income on US Treasury bill holdings backing USDT — the dominant source of profit
  • Returns on other reserve assets: secured loans to institutional borrowers, gold, Bitcoin and corporate bonds
  • Fees associated with token issuance and redemption for institutional counterparties
  • Gains from equity investments (e.g. Northern Data, Rumble, Anchorage Digital, Bitdeer)

What it does not do

  • Tether pays no yield or interest to ordinary USDT holders — the spread between reserve income and zero payout is the core margin
  • Direct retail redemption is not typical; redemptions are generally handled through verified institutional counterparties, with everyday users trading USDT on exchanges instead

Tether reported roughly $13 billion in net profit for 2024, and its KPMG-audited FY2025 financial statements showed reserves exceeding liabilities by $6.814 billion at year-end — though that surplus fell to about $4.1 billion by the Q2 2026 quarterly attestation as gold and Bitcoin valuations moved.

Where it operates

Markets

USDT is used worldwide, with particularly heavy adoption in emerging markets — but Tether's regulatory footprint has narrowed sharply in the European Union.

Where USDT is strongest

  • Global crypto trading — USDT is the most-transacted cryptocurrency and dominant trading-pair currency on exchanges
  • Latin America, Africa and parts of Asia, where it is used for remittances, savings and trade in economies facing currency volatility or dollar scarcity
  • El Salvador — Tether's regulatory home under the Digital Asset Service Provider regime
  • United States — indirectly, through the new USAT token issued under the GENIUS Act framework (USDT itself is not marketed to US retail users)

Where access has narrowed

  • European Union — Tether did not seek the e-money-token authorisation required under MiCA; MiCA-licensed EU exchanges (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken, Crypto.com, Revolut) delisted USDT trading through 2025–2026
  • Individuals can still legally hold and self-custody USDT in the EU — ESMA has clarified the restriction applies to regulated trading venues, not private possession

Assurance

Auditors

Tether's assurance history has moved in 2026 from attestation-only to a hybrid model combining a first full financial-statement audit with ongoing quarterly attestations.

— Tether International, S.A. de C.V. El Salvador — USDT-issuing entity KPMG US (annual audit, FY2025)
— Tether Group Recurring reserve disclosure BDO Italia (quarterly attestation)
— Prior auditor relationship 2017–2018 Friedman LLP (relationship dissolved, no audit completed)

In August 2026, KPMG US completed Tether's first full independent financial-statement audit, covering fiscal year 2025 under US GAAP and AICPA standards, and issued an unqualified ("clean") opinion — including a physical count of Tether's gold bar holdings. PwC assisted with internal-systems preparation ahead of the engagement. Tether continues to publish quarterly reserve attestations — agreed-upon-procedures reports under ISAE 3000 (Revised) — prepared by BDO Italia, a practice in place since 2021 as part of the company's settlements with US regulators. Tether has not published the full KPMG-audited financial statements themselves, only headline figures.

Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Tether has no corporate presence in Malta — no local subsidiary, licence or office has been identified in the sources reviewed. Its relationship with Malta today is regulatory and indirect, running through the EU's MiCA framework, for which Malta's MFSA is a supervisor of crypto-asset service providers.

An indirect, regulation-driven relationship

  • Tether has never sought registration, an e-money-token authorisation, or any other licence from the Malta Financial Services Authority.
  • Because MiCA applies uniformly across the EU, MFSA-supervised crypto-asset service providers — Malta has licensed globally significant names such as OKX, Crypto.com and Bitpanda — are bound by the same July 2026 MiCA deadline that saw USDT delisted from other EU exchanges, since Tether never obtained the required e-money-token authorisation.
  • Malta's long-standing online gaming sector (MGA-licensed operators) has historically been among the more active users of USDT for crypto-denominated deposits and payouts; the EU-wide MiCA delisting from regulated exchanges narrows, though does not eliminate, that channel, since self-custodied USDT remains legal to hold and transfer.

In short, Malta's relevance to Tether is as one of 27 EU jurisdictions enforcing the same stablecoin rulebook, rather than as a place where Tether itself is established, licensed, or headquartered. Any Malta-specific impact would be felt primarily through MFSA-regulated exchanges and Malta-based gaming or fintech businesses that have used USDT, rather than through a direct relationship between Tether and the Maltese regulator.

Professional-services relationship

Relationship with Deloitte

No disclosed relationship identified — but a notable industry contrast

The public sources reviewed for this profile do not show Deloitte acting as Tether's auditor, attestor or disclosed advisor at any point in the company's history. Tether's assurance work has instead been carried out by BDO Italia (quarterly attestations since 2021) and, since August 2026, KPMG US (its first full financial-statement audit).

  • Deloitte's most visible role in the stablecoin sector is as monthly attestor for Circle, the issuer of USDC — Tether's principal competitor — rather than for Tether itself.
  • Tether's own auditor history includes Friedman LLP, whose relationship with the company dissolved in 2018 without a completed audit — again, not a Deloitte engagement.

For a reader tracking the Big Four's exposure to stablecoin issuers, the more relevant Deloitte relationship in this market sits with Circle/USDC, not with Tether. If Deloitte has any non-public advisory relationship with Tether, it is not disclosed in any source reviewed here.
