Overview

What Bitpanda does

Bitpanda is a retail investing and trading platform that lets users buy, sell and hold cryptocurrencies, stocks, ETFs, precious metals and other assets from a single app, starting from as little as €1. Founded in Vienna in December 2014 as Coinimal GmbH by Eric Demuth, Paul Klanschek and Christian Trummer, it rebranded to Bitpanda in 2016 and has grown into an Austrian fintech "unicorn" with over 3,200 tradable instruments.

The group's ultimate parent is Bitpanda Group AG, based in Zurich, Switzerland, beneath which sit the main operating entities: Bitpanda GmbH (Vienna, the core platform), Bitpanda Financial Services GmbH (MiFID II investment firm), Bitpanda Payments GmbH (e-money and PSD2 licence holder), Bitpanda Asset Management GmbH (Berlin, MiCA-licensed for proprietary trading and custody) and Bitpanda Issuance GmbH (Berlin, issues crypto-collateralised bonds). Leadership transitioned in August 2025 when co-founder Paul Klanschek stepped back from day-to-day executive duties into a board advisory role, with Lukas Enzersdorfer-Konrad becoming co-CEO alongside Eric Demuth.

What it sells

Services

Bitpanda's offering spans retail multi-asset trading, staking and savings plans, plus a growing B2B infrastructure business for banks and fintechs.

Crypto trading

Spot buying, selling and swapping of more than 650 cryptocurrencies against euros or other platform assets, available 24/7.

Stocks, ETFs & metals

Fractional trading in over 10,000 stocks and ETFs alongside tokenised precious metals, unified into a single investing app since January 2026.

Staking & savings plans

Staking on more than 50 assets and recurring investment ("savings") plans starting from €1, aimed at long-term retail investors.

Margin trading

Leveraged trading introduced in 2025 across more than 100 crypto assets for more active traders.

Bitpanda Technology Solutions

A white-label, B2B infrastructure product that lets banks and fintechs (Raiffeisen, N26, LBBW, Deutsche Bank) offer branded crypto and digital-asset services to their own customers.

Vision Chain

A regulated Layer-2 blockchain launched March 2026 with the Vision Web3 Foundation, built on Optimism, for European banks and asset managers to issue and settle tokenised assets under MiCA and MiFID II.

How it earns

Revenue streams

Bitpanda earns primarily from trading activity across its retail platform, with a growing institutional/B2B revenue line layered on top.

Core streams

  • Trading fees on crypto, stock, ETF and metals transactions (fees of up to 1.49%, undercutting some larger exchanges)
  • Spreads on asset conversions and swaps
  • B2B licensing and infrastructure fees via Bitpanda Technology Solutions
  • Fees related to margin trading and staking services

Reporting notes

  • Bitpanda reported "operating revenue" of €393 million for FY2024 in its own August 2025 results announcement — more than double the prior year
  • A separate, restated "adjusted revenue" series — prepared ahead of Bitpanda's planned IPO — shows €321 million for FY2024 and €371 million for FY2025 (+16%), on a different accounting basis from the operating-revenue figure above
  • Zero deposit and withdrawal fees across supported payment methods, with monetisation concentrated in trading and spreads rather than account fees

Adjusted EBITDA fell 75% year-on-year in FY2025 (from €52 million to €13 million) despite revenue growth, which the company attributed to deliberate investment in product development, regulatory expansion and international growth ahead of a planned 2026 IPO.

Where it operates

Markets

Bitpanda has built one of the most extensive regulatory footprints of any European crypto platform, with a strategy of securing licences market-by-market rather than relying on a single EU passport.

Corporate & regulatory footprint

  • Austria (Vienna HQ) — FMA-regulated, home MiCA licence (April 2025)
  • Germany — first MiCA licence via BaFin (January 2025), for Bitpanda Asset Management GmbH
  • Malta — MiCA licence from the MFSA (early 2025)
  • United Kingdom — FCA cryptoasset registration (Feb 2025); retail launch 2025
  • United Arab Emirates — VARA broker-dealer licence (March 2025)
  • Offices also in Barcelona, Berlin, Bucharest, Dubai, London, Malta, Milan and Zurich

Reach and expansion

  • MiCA passporting gives Bitpanda access to all 27 EU member states from a base of three national licences
  • 2025 expansion into Latin America and Asia-Pacific institutional partnerships
  • Institutional partners include Raiffeisen, N26, LBBW, Deutsche Bank and the National Bank of Ras Al Khaimah

Assurance

Auditors

Bitpanda's group auditor is KPMG, engaged both for statutory entity-level audits in Austria and Germany and for proof-of-reserves style verification work.

— Bitpanda GmbH Vienna — core operating entity KPMG Austria
— Bitpanda Issuance GmbH Berlin — issues crypto-collateralised bonds KPMG AG Wirtschaftsprüfungsgesellschaft
— Bitpanda Group AG Zurich — ultimate parent; consolidated accounts not publicly filed Not publicly disclosed

In late 2022, following the FTX collapse, Bitpanda engaged KPMG to verify that customer holdings in its five most-traded tokens were fully backed by assets in cold storage. KPMG Austria's 2024 external audit reportedly identified documentation gaps at Bitpanda's Vienna headquarters and flagged "inappropriate privileged" IT access rights held by two shareholders and a board member until revoked in 2025, according to reporting by the International Consortium of Investigative Journalists (ICIJ) based on internal company documents. Separately, internal auditors at Bitpanda's German-licensed subsidiary, Bitpanda Asset Management GmbH, raised information-security concerns in 2025 that echoed earlier findings from a 2023 BaFin audit. Bitpanda has stated that all group companies "have consistently received an unqualified audit opinion" and that it works closely with regulators to address findings.

Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Malta is one of only three jurisdictions where Bitpanda holds a direct MiCA licence, alongside a physical office presence — a materially deeper relationship than a mere market it happens to serve.

A licensed, not just distributed, presence

  • Bitpanda received a MiCA licence from the Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA) in early 2025, its second MiCA authorisation after Germany's BaFin and ahead of its home Austrian licence.
  • The company announced the approval on LinkedIn as reinforcing its position as "Europe's most secure and well-regulated crypto platform."
  • Bitpanda maintains a listed office in Malta alongside its other European locations (Barcelona, Berlin, Bucharest, Dubai, London, Milan, Zurich).

Deputy (now co-) CEO Lukas Enzersdorfer-Konrad has explained the strategy behind holding multiple national MiCA licences, including Malta's, as reflecting genuine differences in how member states implemented and began enforcing MiCA rather than pure redundancy — each authorisation, in his account, adds a layer of resilience and market access rather than duplicating the others. For a market like Malta, with its concentration of MFSA-regulated crypto and gaming businesses, this gives Bitpanda a direct regulatory relationship rather than one mediated solely through EU-wide passporting from elsewhere.
