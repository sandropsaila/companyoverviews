Overview

Two established hubs, two different models

Both countries are major MiCA home states, but they attract different firms: Malta hosts the EU entities of large global exchanges; the Netherlands is dominated by home-grown Dutch platforms and payments champions.

The Dutch AFM issued the EU's first CASP licences on 30 December 2024 (MoonPay, BitStaete, ZBD and Hidden Road) and, with 29 entries, the Netherlands ranks third in the ESMA register behind Germany and France. The Dutch transitional period was the shortest in the EU — legacy DNB registrations lapsed on 30 June 2025, a year before Malta's deadline. Malta's 22 CASPs include OKX, Crypto.com, Bitpanda, Gemini and Gate.

On payments, the Netherlands is the home of iDEAL and of Adyen, Mollie and bunq; it has around 25 operating EMIs. Malta has around 37, many of them EU hubs for foreign groups.

Regulatory landscape

Who supervises what

Malta

  • Single regulator: MFSA for CASPs, EMIs and payment institutions.
  • VFA Act (2018) phased out in favour of MiCA CASP authorisation; transitional period ran to 1 July 2026.
  • EMIs and PIs licensed under the Financial Institutions Act (Cap. 376).

Netherlands

  • CASPs: AFM authorises and supervises conduct; DNB supervises EMT/ART issuers and prudential aspects.
  • EMIs / PIs: DNB under the Wft (Art. 2:10a / 2:3a), with AFM on conduct.
  • Pre-MiCA DNB crypto registration (Wwft, from May 2020) lapsed 30 June 2025 — six-month transition instead of eighteen.

The shorter Dutch transition pushed several international exchanges out of the Netherlands before MiCA (Binance 2023, Gemini 2023, Bitpanda 2024). Some now serve Dutch clients again by passporting a licence from another state, including Malta.

Passport-only or exited

  • Bitpanda — Malta CASP; never DNB-registered and off-boarded Dutch users from 1 Feb 2024. Its former exchange, Bitpanda Pro, now operates independently as One Trading, a Dutch CASP.
  • Gemini — Malta CASP; left the Netherlands in 2023 citing DNB requirements, stating it intended to return under MiCA.
  • OKX and the other Malta CASPs — can passport into the Netherlands; no Dutch entity identified.
  • Dutch CASPs into Malta — Bitvavo, MoonPay, Amdax, Finst, One Trading and others list Malta among their passported host states; none was found with a Malta licence.

What this tells us

  • Overlap is strongest in payments (OpenPayd, Nium, eToro, Crypto.com e-money) rather than crypto exchanges.
  • The Netherlands' early, strict regime drove several Malta-licensed exchanges out; MiCA passporting is now their route back.
  • Dutch pre-MiCA registrations no longer confer operating rights; current status should be checked on the ESMA, AFM and DNB registers.

Opportunity lens (analysis, not sourced fact)

  • Mature Dutch CASP cohort: most Dutch CASPs are one to two years post-authorisation, moving from licensing work to recurring statutory audit, client-asset safeguarding reviews and DORA ICT assessments.
  • Dual-licence firms: MoonPay, ZBD and Fiat Republic combine CASP and EMI/EMT status in the Netherlands, as Crypto.com, Bitpanda, OpenPayd and BVNK do in Malta — scope for combined e-money safeguarding and crypto custody assurance (ISAE 3402 / SOC 1–2, GITC).
  • Cross-border groups: Crypto.com, OpenPayd, Nium and eToro span both countries, suiting coordinated Deloitte Malta – Deloitte Netherlands teams.
  • Independence: firms banking or safeguarding with ING, or connected to bunq, need independence checks given Deloitte's audit roles.
