Overview

What Circle does

Circle issues USDC, a US dollar–pegged stablecoin, and EURC, its euro-pegged counterpart. Unlike Tether, Circle has built its business around being the "regulated, transparent alternative" — publishing detailed, independently attested reserve reports and pursuing licences in every major jurisdiction it operates in, rather than avoiding them.

Founded in 2013 by Jeremy Allaire and Sean Neville, Circle launched USDC in 2018 through the Centre Consortium, a joint venture with Coinbase, before assuming full governance of the stablecoin in 2023. The company redomiciled from the Republic of Ireland to Delaware ahead of its IPO, and went public on the NYSE on 5 June 2025 in the first-ever stablecoin IPO, raising roughly $1.1 billion and closing its debut day at an 168% first-day gain.

What it sells

Services

Circle's product suite centres on USDC itself, plus an expanding set of infrastructure products for institutions building on stablecoins.

USDC & EURC

US dollar and euro–pegged stablecoins, redeemable 1:1, reserves custodied at BNY Mellon and managed by BlackRock via the Circle Reserve Fund.

Circle Mint

The institutional account that lets approved businesses mint and redeem USDC/EURC directly with Circle at zero spread — the primary market for USDC issuance.

Circle Payments Network (CPN)

Launched May 2025, a platform letting financial institutions settle cross-border payments over stablecoins; more than 100 institutions in the pipeline.

Web3 Services & Circle Wallets

Developer tools for deploying smart contracts, managing wallets and cross-chain fund flows, and integrating blockchain payments into applications.

Arc

An open, EVM-compatible Layer-1 blockchain purpose-built for stablecoin finance, with USDC as native gas — Circle's move into owning settlement infrastructure, not just the token.

USYC (via Hashnote)

A tokenised US Treasury product acquired with Circle's 2025 purchase of Hashnote, extending Circle into tokenised money-market instruments for institutional treasury use.

How it earns

Revenue streams

Circle's revenue model closely mirrors Tether's in structure — interest on reserves — but Circle discloses the concentration risk explicitly and is actively diversifying.

Core streams

  • Reserve income: interest earned on the cash and short-dated US Treasuries backing USDC and EURC — historically over 99% of total revenue
  • Other revenue: a fast-growing category covering subscription and services revenue (e.g. blockchain network partnerships) and transaction revenue from value-added network services

What it pays out

  • Distribution costs paid to partners that hold or distribute USDC — over $1 billion in 2024, of which roughly $900 million went to Coinbase alone
  • Circle's own S-1 disclosed that a 1-percentage-point drop in interest rates could remove roughly $441 million of reserve income, underscoring the concentration risk

"Other revenue" (subscription/services plus transaction revenue) grew 252% year-on-year to $24 million in Q2 2025 alone — still small next to reserve income, but the clearest sign of Circle's stated diversification push.

Where it operates

Markets

Circle has pursued licensing in every major market it touches rather than operating from unregulated offshore bases — the opposite posture to Tether.

Regulatory footprint

  • United States — New York State BitLicense (2015), money transmitter licences in 49 states, and compliance with the GENIUS Act (signed July 2025), the first federal US stablecoin law
  • European Union — Circle Mint France SAS holds an Electronic Money Institution licence from France's ACPR (July 2024), making Circle the first major issuer fully cleared for EU retail distribution under MiCA
  • Bermuda — Circle International Bermuda, licensed under the Digital Assets Business Act, handles non-US customer business

Where USDC is used

  • Global crypto trading and DeFi collateral
  • Institutional treasury and cross-border settlement, via Circle Payments Network
  • Consumer and enterprise payments through partners including Visa, Binance, OKX, Corpay, FIS and Fiserv

Assurance

Auditors

Circle runs a dual-track assurance model: full annual financial-statement audits alongside monthly reserve attestations — both currently performed by the same firm.

— Circle Internet Group, Inc. Delaware — NYSE-listed parent, full annual audit Deloitte & Touche LLP
— USDC / EURC reserves Monthly reserve attestation Deloitte & Touche LLP
— Prior auditor 2018–2022 Grant Thornton LLP

Circle switched from Grant Thornton to Deloitte in January 2023 for both engagements — a rare case of one Big Four firm handling both the full-entity PCAOB-standard financial statement audit and the monthly AICPA agreed-upon-procedures reserve attestation for the same stablecoin issuer.

Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Like Tether, Circle has no corporate presence in Malta — but unlike Tether, Circle's EU compliance means USDC and EURC remain fully legal to trade on Malta-regulated exchanges.

Indirect relationship, but on the compliant side of MiCA

  • Circle's EU licence sits with Circle Mint France SAS, authorised as an Electronic Money Institution by France's ACPR — not with any Maltese entity.
  • Because Circle is fully MiCA-authorised, Malta's MFSA-licensed crypto-asset service providers (which include globally significant names such as OKX and Bitpanda) can continue listing and distributing USDC and EURC without the restrictions that hit Tether's USDT.
  • Malta's MGA-licensed online gaming operators — an industry that has used stablecoins for crypto-denominated deposits and payouts — can use USDC through MFSA-regulated exchanges without the MiCA-driven access issues affecting non-compliant stablecoins.

In short, Malta's relevance to Circle is again regulatory rather than corporate — but here it works in Circle's favour: the same MiCA framework that narrowed Tether's EU access is precisely the regime Circle built its compliance strategy around, so Malta-based users and MFSA-licensed platforms face no equivalent disruption in accessing USDC.

Professional-services relationship

Relationship with Deloitte

The strongest Deloitte relationship of any company profiled here

  • Deloitte & Touche LLP has served as Circle's independent registered public accounting firm since January 2023, replacing Grant Thornton, which had audited Circle since 2018.
  • Deloitte performs both of Circle's assurance engagements: the full annual PCAOB-standard audit of Circle's consolidated financial statements (filed with the SEC as part of its 10-K), and the monthly AICPA agreed-upon-procedures attestation of USDC and EURC reserve composition, published at circle.com/transparency.
  • This dual mandate is unusual in the stablecoin sector: most issuers, where they have any external assurance at all, use one firm for periodic attestations and either no auditor or a different one for full financial statements.

For context against the other companies in this index: Deloitte is also Trustly AB (publ)'s statutory auditor in Sweden, but has no known relationship with Tether or Paytently. Circle is the clearest and most extensive Deloitte engagement among the four — both as full-entity auditor and as the ongoing, monthly public face of Circle's reserve transparency.
