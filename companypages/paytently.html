Overview

What Paytently does

Paytently is a payment orchestration platform: rather than processing payments itself end-to-end, it sits between merchants and a curated network of card acquirers, alternative payment methods and banks, using AI-driven routing to send each transaction to whichever partner is most likely to approve it at the lowest cost. It was founded in 2022 by three payments-industry veterans and has grown quickly within specialised, highly regulated verticals — particularly online gaming.

The company is legally structured around Paytently Ltd (its original operating entity) and, since October 2025, Paytently PI Limited (company no. C 113188), which holds the group's Payment Institution licence from the Malta Financial Services Authority (MFSA), licence no. PPI-25221. It also operates a UK entity, Paytently (Paymo UK Ltd), and has staff or offices in Belgium and Portugal in addition to Malta and London.

What it sells

Services

Paytently's offering is organised as a set of payment products plus a dedicated risk service, all accessed through a single API.

Card processing

Acceptance of major card scheme payments across 35+ markets, PCI DSS-compliant, positioned on uptime and approval-rate performance.

Intelligent (AI) routing

Machine-learning-driven transaction routing across 60+ acquirers, with customisable rules and real-time routing strategies to lift acceptance rates and cut cost.

Recurring payments

Subscription billing tooling — automatic billing, one-click payments and smart retries — aimed at reducing involuntary churn.

Open banking

"Paytently Open Banking powered by Mastercard" — an account-to-account payment option launched in 2025 that lets customers pay directly from their bank account at checkout.

Alternative payment methods

Access to 200+ local APMs, including digital wallets, bank transfers, mobile payments and crypto/stablecoin payment options (via a partnership with BVNK).

Digital banking & chargeback mitigation

Digital banking infrastructure for clients, plus a dedicated chargeback mitigation service to reduce dispute-related losses.

How it earns

Revenue streams

As a payment orchestration and, since late 2025, licensed payment institution, Paytently's income is generated primarily from processing merchants' transaction volume rather than software subscriptions.

Core streams

  • Transaction/processing fees on volume routed to acquirers and alternative payment methods
  • Fees for recurring-billing and subscription-management tooling
  • Fees tied to its own regulated payment-institution services, following its October 2025 MFSA licence
  • Value-added services such as chargeback mitigation and fraud/AML tooling (including its SEON partnership)

Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Unlike a foreign company with a Maltese branch, Paytently is a Maltese company at its core: Malta is its registered home, its regulator, and the base from which it has grown.

Malta as home base and regulator

  • Group headquarters at 231 The Adelaide Building, Tower Road, Sliema, Malta — the address used for both Paytently Ltd and Paytently PI Limited.
  • In October 2025, Paytently PI Limited (Company No. C 113188) was granted a Payment Institution licence (No. PPI-25221) by the Malta Financial Services Authority, authorising it to provide regulated payment services across the EU.
  • Silvio Schembri, Malta's Minister for the Economy, Enterprise and Strategic Projects, visited Paytently's headquarters ahead of the licence announcement, publicly citing the company as an example strengthening Malta's reputation as a fintech hub.
  • Paytently's own account of its first year describes operations spanning "Malta, Belgium, London and Portugal" — with Malta named first as the base of the group.

This is a materially different relationship from a multinational simply opening a Maltese office: Paytently's principal regulatory relationship, its registered office, and its licensed operating entity are all Maltese. Its MFSA licence is also what allows it to expand from pure orchestration (routing payments to other licensed institutions) into directly providing regulated payment services in its own right.
