Overview

Two very different profiles

Malta is a licensing hub for international crypto and payment groups; Italy is a large domestic market served mostly by home-grown operators and by passported EU firms.

Malta's MFSA has authorised 22 CASPs under MiCA — including OKX, Crypto.com, Bitpanda, Gemini, Gate and Blockchain.com — placing it among the top five home states in the ESMA register behind Germany, France, the Netherlands and Cyprus. Italy authorised its first CASPs only in 2026; by the end of the MiCA transitional period on 30 June 2026, CONSOB and Banca d'Italia had authorised eight, all Italian firms.

On payments, Malta hosts around 37 operating EMIs, many of them EU hubs for foreign groups (OpenPayd, BVNK, dLocal, eToro, Crypto.com, Bitpanda). Italy's roughly 10 EMIs are dominated by domestic champions such as Nexi Payments and PostePay.

Regulatory landscape

Who supervises what

Malta

  • Single regulator: MFSA for CASPs, EMIs and payment institutions.
  • VFA Act (2018) was an early national crypto regime; now phased out in favour of MiCA CASP authorisation (Markets in Crypto-Assets Act, Cap. 647).
  • EMIs and PIs licensed under the Financial Institutions Act (Cap. 376), with EEA passporting.

Italy

  • CASPs: CONSOB leads authorisation, in coordination with Banca d'Italia (Legislative Decree 129/2024).
  • EMIs / PIs: Banca d'Italia.
  • OAM virtual-currency register (from May 2022) required an Italian office or permanent establishment; the transitional regime ended 30 June 2026 — since 1 July only MiCA-authorised or notified entities may operate.

Practical consequence: pre-MiCA, a Malta-based group needed a separate Italian OAM registration (and Italian presence) to serve Italy. Under MiCA, a Malta CASP can passport into Italy by notification — so "common" presence is increasingly a passporting question rather than a second licence.

Italy — payments

Payment and e-money players

Italy's payments market is concentrated in a handful of large domestic providers.

Nexi Payments S.p.A.

Banca d'Italia EMI authorised 14 Jan 2018; cards and merchant acquiring; passported to 27 EEA states. Selected for the ECB digital euro pilot.

PostePay S.p.A.

Poste Italiane subsidiary; ~28M PostePay cards in circulation. Poste Italiane is also a digital euro pilot participant.

Satispay

Founded 2015 in Milan; card-free A2A mobile payments; 3M+ users and 250k+ merchants. Digital euro pilot participant. Licensing entity: sources conflict — verify.

Numia

Italian payments player selected for the ECB digital euro pilot alongside Nexi, Satispay and Poste.

Tinaba S.p.A.

Italian fintech app; was on the OAM virtual-currency register.

BANCOMAT / MyBank

Domestic card scheme (~36M cards) and bank-owned A2A open-banking scheme — infrastructure rather than licensed EMIs.

Passport-only overlap

  • OKX — never registered with the OAM; serves Italy through its Malta MiCA authorisation (OKX Europe Ltd).
  • OpenPayd — Malta EMI passported to all 30 EEA states, which includes Italy.
  • Nexi Payments — Italian EMI passported to 27 EEA states; Malta coverage not confirmed.

What this tells us

  • The overlap runs one way: international groups licensed in Malta also went into Italy. No Italian-authorised CASP was found holding a Malta licence.
  • Since 1 July 2026 the OAM registrations are no longer the operating basis; these groups now serve Italy on their Malta MiCA passport. The Italian entities remain relevant for local tax, AML history and staff.
  • Italian OAM data is drawn from a published register extract; exact current status of each Italian entity should be checked with OAM / CONSOB.

Opportunity lens (analysis, not sourced fact)

  • First-year MiCA cohort: nine Malta CASPs (May–June 2026) and all eight Italian CASPs were authorised in 2026 — a natural pipeline for first statutory audits, safeguarding / client-asset reviews and ICT (DORA) assessments.
  • Assurance reports: CASP custody and EMI safeguarding models lend themselves to ISAE 3402 / SOC 1–2 and GITC work, as do outsourced platforms serving several licence holders.
  • Cross-border groups: the six common groups have Malta and Italy footprints, suiting coordinated Deloitte Malta – Deloitte Italy engagement teams.
  • Independence: any pursuit of Poste group entities (PostePay, and Conio via its shareholding) needs independence checks given Deloitte's Group-auditor role.
