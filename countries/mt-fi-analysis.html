Overview

An international hub and a small domestic market

Malta licenses the EU arms of global exchanges; Finland's crypto market is served by five home-grown CASPs and by global platforms passporting in — several of them from Malta.

Finland ran one of the shortest MiCA transitions in the EU: legacy VASP registrations lapsed on 30 June 2025, a year before Malta's deadline. By then the FIN-FSA had begun authorising its own CASPs — Coinmotion first, followed by Tesseract, Bittimaatti, NorthCrypto and Kvarn Capital. Finland's standout contribution is on stablecoins: Paxos Issuance Europe Oy, a Finnish EMI, issues USDG, among the first MiCA-compliant US-dollar stablecoins in the EU.

Regulatory landscape

Who supervises what

Malta

  • MFSA licenses CASPs, EMIs and PIs; FIAU supervises AML.
  • VFA Act (2018) replaced by MiCA; transitional period ran to 1 July 2026.

Finland

  • FIN-FSA is the single licensing and supervisory authority for CASPs, PIs and EMIs, and enforces AML.
  • National Act on Crypto-Asset Service Providers implements MiCA; the earlier VASP Act (2019) was repealed.
  • Six-month transition ended 30 June 2025. Updated AML guidance from 1 July 2025 brings CASPs explicitly into scope.
  • CASPs handling fiat or stablecoin payments need a separate PI/EMI licence or a licensed partner — Finnish CASPs largely hold dual authorisation.

Finland — payments

FIN-FSA-licensed payment players

Finland's e-money sector is small (8 EMIs) but notable for stablecoin issuance and card infrastructure.

EMI (formerly Membrane Finance, licensed Jan 2022; acquired by Paxos 2024). Issues USDG and EUROe, MiCA-compliant EMTs passported across the EEA.

How the two markets connect

No group was found with its own licensed entity in both Malta and Finland. The connection runs through MiCA and e-money passporting — and through a Finnish stablecoin used by a Malta-licensed exchange.

What this tells us

  • Unlike Italy and the Netherlands, Finland has no history of Malta-licensed groups opening local entities; they serve Finns purely by passport.
  • Flow is two-way but asymmetric: large Malta exchanges into Finland; only two small Finnish CASPs passport into Malta.
  • The strongest structural link is stablecoins — Finland issues USDG, Malta-licensed exchanges distribute it.

Opportunity lens (analysis, not sourced fact)

  • Stablecoin issuer assurance: EMT issuers such as Paxos Issuance Europe need reserve reporting and safeguarding assurance comparable to Deloitte's Circle work.
  • Dual-licence Finnish CASPs: Coinmotion (CASP + PI) mirrors the OKX and Gate model in Malta — combined custody, safeguarding and DORA ICT scope.
  • Cross-border coordination: Malta exchanges' Finnish customer base and Finnish CASPs' Malta passports suit joint Deloitte Malta – Deloitte Finland teams, subject to independence checks.
