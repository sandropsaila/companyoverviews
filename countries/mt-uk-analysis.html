Overview

Two regimes, no passport

The UK sits outside MiCA, so a Malta licence gives no access to UK clients and vice versa. Groups that want both markets must hold separate licensed entities — which makes the overlap here unusually concrete.

UK crypto firms are today only registered with the FCA for anti-money-laundering purposes; the FCA has approved only a small minority of applicants since 2020. That changes now: the FCA's authorisation gateway opens on 30 September 2026 and closes on 28 February 2027, and the new FSMA regime starts on 25 October 2027. Existing registrations do not carry over. On payments, London remains a hub for EMIs, and several Malta-licensed groups — OpenPayd, BVNK, Nium — run their UK and EU businesses side by side from the two jurisdictions.

Regulatory landscape

Who supervises what

Malta

  • MFSA licenses CASPs (MiCA), EMIs and PIs; FIAU supervises AML.
  • MiCA licence passports across the EEA — but not into the UK.

United Kingdom

  • FCA: crypto registration under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (AML only); EMIs under the EMRs 2011; PIs under the PSRs 2017.
  • SI 2026/102 (made 4 Feb 2026) brings cryptoassets into the FSMA perimeter; FCA final rules published 30 Jun 2026.
  • Gateway: 30 Sep 2026 – 28 Feb 2027. New regime in force 25 Oct 2027.
  • Retail ban on crypto derivatives remains; the crypto ETN ban was lifted in late 2025.

What this tells us

  • The strongest overlap of any country pair: without passporting, firms that serve both markets must build two regulated entities.
  • London hosts the group base of OpenPayd and Blockchain.com while Malta is the EU licensing base — the post-Brexit "UK + EU hub" model.
  • Every UK crypto registration above must be re-applied for under the new regime by 28 February 2027.
