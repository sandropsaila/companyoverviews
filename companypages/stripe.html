Overview

What Stripe does

Stripe builds the software and APIs that let internet businesses accept payments and run the financial side of their operations — from a single developer integrating a checkout page to enterprises processing billions in volume. Founded in 2010 by Irish brothers Patrick and John Collison, the company grew from a simple "accept a credit card online" tool into a broad financial-infrastructure platform spanning billing, fraud prevention, card issuing, business financing, tax automation, and — more recently — stablecoin payments.

Stripe is dual-headquartered in South San Francisco and Dublin, with the Irish entity structure (commonly cited as Stripe Technology Company Limited, with operating subsidiaries including Stripe Payments Europe, Limited and Stripe Technology Europe, Limited) serving as its hub for European and EEA-wide regulation. The company remains private: rather than pursue an IPO, it has used a series of employee tender offers — most recently valuing it at $159 billion in February 2026 — to provide liquidity while the Collison brothers retain majority voting control through a multi-class share structure.

What it sells

Services

Stripe's product suite spans core payment processing and a growing set of adjacent financial-infrastructure tools.

Stripe Payments

Core payment-processing APIs supporting 135+ currencies and 100+ payment methods, used for online, in-app and in-person transactions.

Billing & Connect

Subscription and recurring-revenue management (Billing), and infrastructure for marketplaces and platforms to route payments between multiple parties (Connect).

Radar & Issuing

Machine-learning fraud prevention (Radar) and an end-to-end platform for issuing physical and virtual cards, certified with Visa and Mastercard (Issuing).

Capital & Treasury

Merchant cash advances and term loans via bank partners (Capital), and embedded banking-as-a-service functionality for platforms (Treasury).

Tax, Sigma & Atlas

Automated sales-tax calculation (Tax), a SQL-based analytics environment over a business's own Stripe data (Sigma), and a service for remotely incorporating a US company (Atlas).

Stablecoins & Tempo

Stablecoin pay-ins (re-enabled 2024), the $1.1 billion acquisition of stablecoin platform Bridge (closed Feb 2025), crypto wallet provider Privy (acquired June 2025), and the Tempo blockchain built with Paradigm for stablecoin settlement.

How it earns

Revenue streams

Stripe's core model is a usage-linked transaction fee, supplemented by a growing suite of software products sold on top of the payments layer.

Core streams

  • Per-transaction processing fees — typically around 2.9% + $0.30 for US card payments, varying by country, card type and negotiated volume discounts
  • Custom, interchange-plus pricing for large enterprise customers
  • Fees for value-added products: Billing, Issuing, Capital, Tax, Treasury and Connect

Scale and mix

  • Stripe's combined "revenue suite" (Billing, Invoicing and Tax) was on track for a $1 billion annual run rate in 2026, per the company's own 2025 annual letter
  • Stablecoin payment volume on the platform roughly doubled to approximately $400 billion in 2025
  • The company has described itself as "robustly profitable" since 2024, with free cash flow reported at approximately $2.2 billion in 2024

As a private company, Stripe does not publish a line-item revenue breakdown; the transaction-fee and software-fee split above is inferred from its own product disclosures and press coverage rather than a filed financial statement.

Where it operates

Markets

Stripe serves businesses across dozens of countries, regulated in the EU through its Irish entity and in the US through money-transmission and banking-partner arrangements.

Regulatory footprint

  • European Union / EEA — regulated via Stripe Technology Europe, Limited, authorised as an Electronic Money Institution by the Central Bank of Ireland, passporting across all EEA states under PSD2
  • Ireland also hosts Stripe Payments Europe, Limited and other group entities as part of its EU operating base
  • United States — operates under state-level money-transmission frameworks and banking partnerships (e.g. for Capital's term loans)
  • Local acquiring and currency support across 46+ countries, including Australia, Brazil, Singapore, Japan and India

Key customers & verticals

  • Powers checkout and billing for Amazon, Shopify, OpenAI, Anthropic, Airbnb, Meta and BMW, among others
  • Reported to process payments for 90% of the Dow Jones Industrial Average and 80% of the Nasdaq 100 by customer count or volume
  • Positioned itself as infrastructure for "agentic commerce," partnering with OpenAI on Instant Checkout inside ChatGPT

Auditors

Stripe's published SOC 3 report for the year to 30 September 2025 was issued by Linford & Company LLP, a Denver/Louisville, Colorado-based CPA firm specialising in SOC, HIPAA and FedRAMP examinations — founded by former Big Four auditors but not itself one of the Big Four. Regional relationship

Relationship with Malta

Stripe serves Malta as one of its European markets, reached through its Irish regulatory base rather than through a dedicated Maltese licence or entity.

Malta as a served market, not a licensed one

  • Stripe expanded into Malta in 2020, alongside four other new European markets — the Czech Republic, Romania, Bulgaria and Cyprus.
  • Stripe's EU/EEA regulatory status runs through its Irish entity, Stripe Technology Europe, Limited, an Electronic Money Institution authorised by the Central Bank of Ireland and passported across all EEA states, Malta included, under PSD2 "freedom of services" rules.
  • No evidence was found of a separate Malta-registered Stripe entity or an MFSA licence — unlike several other companies in this index (Bitpanda, OKX, Blockchain.com, Trumia), Stripe has not sought a dedicated Maltese authorisation.

Stripe's own public FAQ on Connect and PSD2 explicitly addresses this model: businesses in any EEA country, including Malta, are told that Stripe's Irish licence gives it "equivalent regulatory status as a firm authorised in your country" — a passporting-based relationship rather than a direct one. This makes Stripe's Malta relationship lighter in form than the dedicated MFSA-licensed entities several crypto and payments firms in this index have established, even though Maltese merchants and consumers can use Stripe's products without restriction.
